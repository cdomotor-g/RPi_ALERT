RTL-SDR sticks

Each stick hears every channel in its window at once — a slice of the band — and each channel is a receiver of its own in Flood-Net. The window is what a stick costs this Pi: every channel's decoder takes in the whole of it, so a wider one is more work for each channel and hears none of them better. Automatic takes the narrowest that holds the stick's channels, kept between No narrower than and No wider than: a widest keeps a stick light whatever it is given (a channel it cannot hold is not decoded, and says so), a narrowest shows more of the band on the waterfall. Or choose one window exactly.

Defaults — what a stick with nothing of its own uses, and each new stick

A stick's field marked default follows these; change it on the stick and it is that stick's own, until ↺ takes it back.

Decoding — ALERT2, the decoder, between bursts

Lean filters a burst once for each carrier guess instead of once for every small nudge around it. At the first base station to compare the two, 14 of 11,628 live bursts read differently (0.12%), and lean took a third of the time. Full goes back to the full decoder; Compare sends the full decoder's readings and counts on each stick's card how often lean agreed. Light filters the burst down to 48 kHz before trying its carrier guesses: about half of lean's time again, and in Flood-Net's tests as many true readings; Compare light sends lean's readings and counts how often light agreed — how light is proven here before it is the default.

Each channel's decoder filters the stick's whole window into its channel, burst or not, and between bursts that is nearly all of its work. Only around bursts looks at the channel 25 times a second instead and filters only from a second before something shows: from 2½ times less work in a 240 kHz window to 20 times less at 2.4 MHz — but its first live check missed some of the weakest bursts, ones just over the squelch. Check runs both and counts, on each stick's card, readings only one of them got; the readings sent are the first's.

Serial devices

Quansheng radios, ERT-A2 receivers and GPS units are recognised from what they send. Set a port's type here only if it is not.

Recordings

Keep the IQ of every burst heard on these channels — to see what is on one that nothing here decodes yet (ALERT2, another format), and to build the decoder that will. Each is the channel's own 240 ksps, as .iq8 with a .json beside it saying what it was; Flood-Net's SDR card and its tests read them as they are. The newest are kept, up to the size below; the Bursts tab lists them.

What Flood-Net may do here

This base station checks in with Flood-Net's Base Stations tab once a minute — Flood-Net never connects to it, and there is nothing here for anyone else to connect to. Flood-Net's administrators see its health and, if you allow it, change its settings, restart it and install updates. Never from there: the ingest token, where readings go, the web page's password, SSH keys, or this choice — it is made here. What they asked is on the Requests tab.

SSH access

Log in as alert with an SSH key on this list — the same user name on every RPi ALERT, and no password to forget or pass on. Each key is one person's: take it off the list when they move on. Keys come only from the SD card, sudo rpi-alert access add-key, or the GitHub accounts and Flood-Net team keys you choose to accept — never from this page or from Flood-Net's requests.

Loading…

Adding a key — and getting in when nobody can
  • On the SD card: a line ssh_key = ssh-ed25519 AAAA… you@laptop in rpi-alert.conf on its bootfs drive (the set-up page writes it). The card's lines replace the ones before.
  • A team's GitHub accounts: ssh_github = name, name on the card — their public keys, fetched every hour.
  • Over SSH, with sudo: sudo rpi-alert access add-key "ssh-ed25519 AAAA… you@laptop"; rpi-alert access lists them.
  • Nobody can get in: put alert_password = … in rpi-alert.conf on the card, boot, and log in as alert at the console (or over SSH, if password login is on). It is wiped from the card once applied.
System and updates

Software updates

Updates install over the network: no need to re-flash the SD card. Settings, the queue and the receiver ids are kept, and if a new version does not start, the one before it is put back.
